Prototype review · 5 October 2026

MicrobeAtlas metadata → ontology terms

The prototype is scientifically useful as a closed-vocabulary, calibrated label-transfer system. It is not yet evidence that exact ontology mapping generalises beyond Metalog. The strongest result is reliable ranking and selective prediction; the largest risk is habitat coverage and label convention shift.

3,437,058 Atlas samples with keyword text 58,365 Atlas↔Metalog links 17,720 capped training samples ENVO · Uberon · PO · FOODON Study-grouped 5-fold CV

What works

GPT-derived keyword and sub-biome embeddings transfer common Metalog labels substantially better than TF–IDF or zero-shot term retrieval. Calibrated hierarchy back-off trades coverage for accuracy and confidence ranks errors well.

What remains unproven

Exact-term accuracy on new habitat families. The external set has coarse biome labels only; open-vocabulary unseen-label recovery is near zero for the supervised models.

Recommendation

Present the system as calibrated ontology label transfer, not general ontology understanding. Ship the term, probability, ancestor/back-off decision, coverage flag and control flag together. Do not claim the 90% exact target outside Metalog until an exact-term external benchmark exists.

One sample, concretely

isolation_source: feces; host: Homo sapiens

Upstream extraction might yield keywords {feces, gut, human} and sub-biome human gut. Each is embedded and L2-normalised; the two blocks receive equal weight. Three slot-specific models then predict, for example, biome animal-associated environment, feature intestine environment, and material fecal material.

Recommended model

A class prototype blends the centred term vector with the centroid of training samples carrying that term, then adds a log label-frequency prior. This corrects the sample/term “modality gap” and Metalog’s conventional preference for a few labels.

Important: the final model is closed-vocabulary. “Open” prototype variants almost never recover a label absent from the training fold.

Coverage detector

8.6% of the Atlas is flagged outside Metalog-like coverage. On the external gold set, 18.8% is flagged; rates are ~32% for plant/other versus ~5–6% for soil/water.

The flag is useful provenance, but probability already captures much of the same signal in Metalog. Publish both; do not count flagged feature/material answers toward the 90% claim.

LLM reranking

The 500-biome pilot increases nested-CV coverage from 43.0% to 51.2%, but strict accuracy falls from 90.2% to 89.1%. Feature gets no gain; material improves in the pilot but is disabled in the saved production parameters.

Conclusion: an interesting targeted experiment, not a demonstrated production improvement.

Repository shape after review

scripts/ontology_mapping/ now contains one numbered seven-step pipeline, two shared modules, analyses, and an isolated experiments/ folder. Fast regression tests live in tests/test_ontology_mapping.py. Detailed future work is in ontology-mapping-future-work.md.

Data and labels

Is Metalog gold truth? Newcomer

No. It is unusually valuable manually curated silver data. Curators resolve submission errors and map to standard vocabularies, but ontologies change and reasonable curators can choose different granularities.

Why only 58,365 linked samples if Metalog has ~159k? Newcomer

The join requires a shared sample/BioSample/SRA accession in the current Atlas metadata. The 2026-09-20 files contain 159,310 Metalog rows, but only 57,874 distinct Metalog samples link to 58,365 Atlas records.

Why cap each study at 50 samples? Engineer

Large cohorts contain many near-duplicate samples and otherwise dominate both fitting and scoring. The cap reduces this bias, but still weights a 50-sample study more than a five-sample study; study-level metrics should also be reported.

Are duplicate texts independent observations? Expert

No. Gold evaluation keeps the first copy per study, while training retains duplicates because deduplicating slightly hurt performance. Bootstrap units and folds must remain studies/projects, never samples.

Do label remaps leak test information? Expert

The draft biome map uses aggregate model/ontology evidence and then is evaluated on the same research corpus, so it is not a pristine external intervention. It needs curator review and versioning before confirmatory evaluation.

Ontologies and targets

Why three labels per sample? Newcomer

They answer different questions: broad biome, local environmental feature/anatomy, and sampled material. For a gut sample those may be animal-associated environment, intestine environment and fecal material.

Why add PO and FOODON beyond ENVO/Uberon? Engineer

Metalog already uses them for leaves and foods. Retaining them restored 465 linked feature labels and 128 material labels, with strong plant-material improvements.

Why omit definitions from the default term text? Expert

In these experiments, sentence-length definitions moved term embeddings away from short keyword lists and hurt zero-shot retrieval. This is empirical, encoder-specific and should be revisited with a term/sample dual encoder.

Does a parent prediction count as correct? Expert

Exact, strict hierarchical and lenient hierarchical outcomes are reported separately. Strict accepts gold or a true ancestor; lenient also accepts a descendant because Metalog may have stopped at a coarser term.

Are all predicted slot combinations valid? Expert

Not guaranteed. Slot models are independent and the current parser does not enforce OWL domain/range or cross-slot constraints. Add explicit validity checks before downstream scientific use.

Embeddings and models

What do embeddings add over TF–IDF? Newcomer

They capture semantic similarity when metadata and ontology labels use different words. Keyword+sub-biome linear improves raw biome/feature/material exact top-1 from 0.455/0.611/0.680 to 0.526/0.631/0.707.

Why embed LLM-extracted keywords instead of raw metadata? Engineer

The extraction removes IDs and study boilerplate and compresses heterogeneous fields into a shared style. But it introduces model cost, prompt/version dependence and possible hallucination; the TF–IDF raw-text baseline is therefore essential.

Why does zero-shot retrieval perform poorly? Expert

Sample keyword lists and ontology names occupy different embedding regions, label priors are extreme, and many terms are near synonyms. Centring and priors help, but direct open retrieval remains only 0.028/0.136/0.083 exact top-1.

Why prefer prototype over linear? Expert

It is interpretable, uses term semantics and is less affected by related-study leakage. It improves material and back-off coverage, but linear is better on exact biome top-1; neither dominates every slot.

Can the same embeddings support other research? Expert

Yes—coverage, clustering, retrieval, active learning and study similarity—but validate each use separately. A representation good for frequent-label transfer is not automatically biologically faithful.

Evaluation and calibration

Why group folds by study/project? Newcomer

Samples in one study share metadata templates and wording. Random sample splits would reward memorising those templates. Project grouping also merges related studies that share accessions or sampling programmes.

What does “90% target” mean? Engineer

On held-out Metalog folds, among answered predictions after hierarchical back-off, strict accuracy is targeted at 90%. It is not a guarantee for every class or for the external Atlas population.

Is calibration selection unbiased? Expert

Temperature and tau are cross-fitted, which is sound. Model/hyperparameter choices were informed by many experiments on the same corpus, so final claims still need one frozen external exact-term test.

Why are macro scores so low? Expert

Hundreds of rare labels have few studies, while fecal/intestine terms dominate micro accuracy. Macro accuracy exposes this and should be co-primary, alongside per-label support and confidence intervals.

Does the coverage flag add value beyond probability? Expert

On Metalog, little incremental AUC. Outside Metalog, it identifies underrepresented plant/food/lab habitats and is valuable provenance. Treat it as a domain-shift warning, not a calibrated correctness probability.

Deployment and reranking

What should downstream users receive? Newcomer

Raw top-1, calibrated probability, selected ancestor or abstention, candidate list, coverage flag, control flag, ontology/model versions and provenance. Never publish a bare term as certain truth.

What happens to controls and mock communities? Engineer

They are flagged before publication because they have no natural habitat. Current rules flag 13,204 Atlas samples; some real laboratory microcosms need manual policy decisions.

Did LLM reranking help? Expert

Not conclusively. The biome pilot trades a modest coverage gain for accuracy just below target; feature is off; material looked promising but remains disabled. A larger project-held-out pilot is required.

Is the pipeline reproducible across machines? Expert

Core predictions are effectively reproducible, and the corrected step 5/6 math now matches. Exact floating ties can still reorder a handful of diagnostic CV predictions; run manifests and pinned package versions should be added to releases.

What is the next go/no-go test? Expert

A 1,000-sample, exact-term, double-curated benchmark from unseen projects and weak habitats. If calibrated precision and rare-label recall hold there, scale active learning; if not, prioritise new labels and constrained retrieval over a larger model.
